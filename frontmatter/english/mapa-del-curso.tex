\section*{The year at a glance}

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}c c c c X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Part} & \textbf{Days} & \textbf{Season} & \textbf{Numbers} & \textbf{What happens} \\
\midrule
1 & 1--65 & autumn & 0 to 10 & A new number every week: traced,
counted, coloured and found. Lucía's birthday has seven candles, and
Christmas ends the part. \\
2 & 66--130 & winter & up to 20 & Ten, and the numbers from 11 to 20;
more, fewer and the same; the number before and the number after; the
first additions, putting pictures together. \\
3 & 131--195 & spring & up to 50 & Adding and taking away up to 10;
splitting a number in two; counting in twos, fives and tens; thirteen
or thirty? \\
4 & 196--260 & summer & up to 100 & The tens up to 100; adding and
taking away up to 20; money, time and measuring, in Grandma Rosa's
village. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\vspace{6mm}
Each part has 13 weeks of 5 days -- holidays included: the book is still
a page a day, Monday to Friday, at Christmas and in the summer, like
\emph{First Words}. A new number does not arrive until the one before
has been traced, counted, coloured and found for a whole week.
